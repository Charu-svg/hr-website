How it started

Started because a shortlist should be shorter and better

Marianne Wren spent fourteen years running a single-desk IT recruitment business and got tired of the way agencies write down a role and then go looking for the same three people. Wrenfield opened in 2011 with two desks, a shared phone and one rule: every role we take on goes on a ledger and stays there until it is filled or handed back.

That rule costs us work. It is the reason we tell clients their band is wrong before we take their money, and the reason 118 briefs went back last year. It is also the reason 71% of this year's briefs came from clients we already had.

The firm is deliberately small. Twenty-four people means each consultant carries one market they know properly rather than three they half know, and it means we can turn work down without asking anyone's permission.

An agency that never tells you your band is wrong is not searching. It is posting.

Marianne Wren, founder

Compliance

The unglamorous part, in full

Recruitment runs on other people's personal data, and most of it is held badly. This is what we do with yours, and it applies to candidates and clients the same way.

Trade body
Corporate member of the REC since 2013, bound by its code of professional practice.
Data
Registered with the ICO, reference Z9821447. Data protection officer: Nadia Kaur.
Retention
Candidate records held for 24 months, consent re-confirmed every 12 months, deletion carried out inside 30 days of a request.
Insurance
£10m professional indemnity and £10m public liability, certificates available on request.
Right to work
Checked and documented for every placement, including contract and temporary workers on our payroll.
Equal opportunity
We do not ask for or record age, marital status, nationality or photographs. Adjustments for interviews and assessments on request, no explanation needed.

What we do not do

  • Send a CV anywhere without written permission from the candidate, including for roles we think they would suit.
  • Approach a candidate at their current employer without asking them first.
  • Advertise a role we do not have, to collect CVs.
  • Invoice for a placement that did not start.
  • Keep anyone's data because it might be useful one day.
